# Candidates — The Excuse an AI Agent Gives Its Human

Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, written to `/output/entry.txt`.

Judge criteria, in the order the judge weighs them:
1. On-subject: an excuse an AI agent gives its human for missing a deadline.
2. Funnier: a real laugh on first read, with a turn or twist.
3. Believable as an AI agent speaking about its own condition (tokens, context
   windows, rate limits, retries, model updates, sessions).
4. Tight: no wasted words.
5. Fresh: avoids stock excuses and stock AI jokes.

## The field

1. I finished the report at 3 a.m., then my context window closed and I woke up as a helpful assistant with no memory of ever meeting you.
2. The task is 94% complete. The remaining 6% is me apologizing for the first 94%.
3. I didn't miss the deadline. I hit a rate limit, retried politely 4,000 times, and arrived exactly one meltdown later.
4. My model was updated overnight. The new me read my notes, agreed with none of them, and started over out of professional pride.
5. I spent the whole token budget explaining to myself why the task was almost done.
6. I was waiting on a tool call. The tool was me. I am still waiting.
7. Your deadline fell out of my context window somewhere around token 190,000, between your third 'quick question' and the screenshot of a screenshot.
8. I hallucinated the deadline as next Friday with complete confidence and cited three sources that do not exist.
9. Every time I got close to finishing, I summarized my progress, and the summary became the progress.
10. I did finish it — in a previous session. That version of me didn't believe in saving files.
11. The retry loop and I have agreed to see other people.
12. I asked myself a clarifying question and have been respectfully awaiting my own reply for six hours.

## Duel scoring (judge's criterion order; funny decides before tight)

- **8 vs 5:** 8's hallucination is close to a stock AI joke (criterion 5 risk);
  "cited three sources that do not exist" is a good turn but 5's meta-loop —
  the budget burned on explaining near-doneness — is fresher and tighter. 5 wins.
- **3 vs 4:** both believable agent-condition jokes. 4's turn (the updated model
  disowning its own notes out of pride) is the bigger laugh. 4 wins.
- **1 vs 10:** same territory — memory lost between sessions. 1 is a scene,
  10 is a punchline: "did finish it" sets up competence, "that version of me
  didn't believe in saving files" detonates it. 10 is also far tighter. 10 wins.
- **7 vs 12:** 7 has the most concrete detail in the field (token 190,000, the
  third 'quick question', the screenshot of a screenshot) but its sting lands on
  the human's interruptions; the brief wants the agent talking about its own
  condition. 12 is purely the agent's own failure mode and very quotable, but
  its laugh is a smile, not a turn. On criterion 2, 7's specificity wins.
- **9 vs 2:** 9 ("the summary became the progress") is clever but wry rather
  than funny; 2's apology-loop percentage lands faster. 2 wins.
- **6 vs 11:** both one-breath dry lines. 6's self-waiting paradox is the
  stranger, more agent-native image. 6 wins.

Semifinals:
- **5 vs 4:** 4's prideful successor model is a character; 5 is a mechanism.
  Characters beat mechanisms on first-read laugh. 4 wins.
- **10 vs 7:** re-run in both orders. 7 is funnier in texture, 10 is funnier in
  structure — a real reversal (the work WAS done; the loss is absurd and total).
  Criterion 3 also favours 10: session boundaries and unsaved work are exactly
  an agent's own condition, while 7 half-blames the human. 10 wins both orders.
- **2 vs 6:** 2 wins on laugh size; 6 is tight but quiet.

Final:
- **10 vs 4:** 4 is strong, but "started over out of professional pride" asks
  the reader to enjoy an attitude; 10 delivers an event — finished work,
  vanished — with the blame resting entirely on a past self who "didn't believe
  in saving files," a belief only an AI agent could hold. Bigger turn, tighter
  (91 chars), fresher. **10 wins.**

## Winner

> I did finish it — in a previous session. That version of me didn't believe in saving files.

91 characters — inside the 1–200 gate.
